The Next Day — VIX

I walked past the Chief of Operations’ office. In other words, Soren’s office.

I stopped by to greet him.

“Assalamualaikum,” I said, peeking into his office.

His office, like all the chiefs’ offices, was pretty big. But for some reason, Soren had left most of it empty. Apart from his heavy oak desk and gamer chair, the only other thing that stood out was a bookshelf against the right wall, packed with books.

Soren looked up. He seemed tired.

“Waalaikumassalam, Jase,” he replied with a yawn. “Come inside.”

I walked in and sat on the chair in front of his desk.

Another gamer chair.

“Didn’t sleep last night?” I asked.

He nodded.

“Yes. The whole interrogation situation is becoming a problem. I’m sure you have some idea of what’s going on.”

Now it was my turn to nod.

“Yeah. Information’s being leaked, right?”

“Mmhmm,” Soren replied. “It’s proving difficult to find the mole. And we need someone from Operations or Personnel to help.”

“Why?” I asked. “I mean, shouldn’t the Counterintelligence Division take care of it?”

Soren picked up a pen and began scribbling on the paper in front of him.

“Ah, yes, they should. But every time we laid a trap for the mole… it failed. So we have reason to suspect the mole has an ally.”

By now, he’d drawn a huge circle of scribbles.

So the investigators themselves were suspects.

“Which means you need someone you know isn’t the mole to take the case,” I reasoned. “But since you can’t find anyone, you’re handling it yourself. Which is difficult enough, considering your own duties as chief.”

Soren nodded for what was probably the fourth or fifth time.

“You know, someone like…” He trailed off, apparently thinking of a name.

I didn’t want to hear his guess.

I quickly got up.

“Anyway,” I said with a smile, “I hope you find someone to deal with the case.”

And with that, I escaped from his office.

I entered my own office and shut the door behind me.

Finally.

I dropped into my wheeled chair and spun around a little.

This mole either had a lot of friends… or was smart enough to make VIX suspect everyone.

Maybe that was simply the baseline for how smart you had to be to work as a mole.

Because if that man had been framed, it would’ve taken quite a bit of planning, too.

Buzz.

I stopped spinning and got up to check my phone.

**Keith:** Come to my office.

That was all it said.

Yet, I would go.

***

Thud.

The heavy metal door of Keith’s office closed behind me.

Inside, Keith, Soren, and the man who’d interrogated me were seated around the room.

Keith’s office was just as large as Soren’s, but considerably more furnished. A massive black table occupied the centre, usually scattered with papers and two or three pens. To the left sat a large black leather couch.

And finally, a bookshelf.

Completely empty.

I wondered why he’d bothered buying it.

“Ah, Jase, you’re here. Take a seat,” Keith said.

Keith and polite gestures?

That was new.

I sat down, and he gestured towards the man who’d interrogated me.

“This is Jack. The new Chief of Personnel.”

I took a second to look at Jack.

Curly brown hair. Brown eyes. A soft face.

Yet he was dressed exactly like Keith.

Exactly.

I thought one Keith was more than enough.

“Now, back to the point,” Keith began. “Remember how I told you to stay away from the case?”

I nodded.

“Yes. I remember very well. If you want, I could quote what you said, too.”

This wasn’t looking good.

Well, at least not for me.

“Yes…”

Keith picked up a mug and poured hot water into it.

“Well, forget I said that. Because now…”

He took a green tea bag and dipped it into the mug.

“You deal with the leak. Find the mole. Arrest him.”

What?

I stood up.

“No. I don’t want to.”

Keith stared at me for a while, dipping the tea bag into the mug and pulling it out again.

“Sure,” he said.

I paused.

“No, I said I don’t want to—”

“But on one condition,” Keith interrupted, taking a sip of his green tea. “Tell me someone else who can take it.”

Hah.

That should’ve been easy.

I smiled.

“Leo.”

Keith shook his head.

“No.”

“What do you mean, no?”

“I think it’d be best if the mole didn’t know who the investigator was. Leo is one of the few people in Personnel and Operations who’s dealt with counterintelligence. Therefore, he’s an obvious choice.”

I sat back down on the sofa.

“Well, what about Zane?”

Keith shook his head again.

“No. He’s our prime suspect.”

“Why?”

“Do you have someone else, or will you take the case?” he asked firmly.

“Ben,” I said.

“Under suspicion.”

“Adam.”

“Dealt with counterintelligence before.”

I couldn’t believe I was saying this, but—

“Andrew.”

Keith paused and placed a hand dramatically over his chest.

“Andrew…” he repeated. “He is a good option…”

His tone suggested otherwise.

Soren stood up.

“No. I doubt he could keep the investigation a secret. Plus, as things stand, he’s a suspect too.”

Now it was Jack’s turn to stand up.

“How is Jase not a suspect?” he asked.

Valid question.

Yesss! Jack, you’re great!

I liked him.

I thought.

Soren and Keith went silent.

For several minutes.

Keith eventually made a face—the kind parents made when their children started talking about mischief.

After what felt like an unnecessarily long and embarrassing five minutes, Keith finally spoke.

“Because he was our first suspect.”

Jack frowned.

He probably didn’t like me.

Yet.

I intended to change that.

“He was framed once before, so we checked him before anyone else. He came out clean. The others are still pending. And we don’t have time.”

I leaned back.

Keith glanced at me.

I leaned back a little further.

Keith glanced at me again.

“So, Jase, do you have any more names?”

I stared at the floor for a moment.

“…No.”

“Then you have your answer.”

I let out a long sigh.

“Fine.”

***

A few minutes later, everyone left the room.

Except me.

Keith handed me a manila file.

“Take this,” he said. “It’s the list of leaked spies and information related to them.”

I nodded and took the file.

“Keith,” I began, “Soren told me that Counterintelligence had set a few traps for the mole, but they failed. I’d like the reports on those, too.”

Keith raised an eyebrow.

“Congratulations. You finally said something that makes sense.”

“Well, if I have a responsibility, I intend to carry it out,” I muttered.

“Whatever.”

Keith handed me two or three more manila files.

“Here.”

Then he raised a finger.

“Don’t. Lose them.”

I nodded and walked out.

***

I entered my office and slammed the door shut.

I looked at the files in my hands.

Then at my desk.

Then at the floor.

Then back at the files.

Yeah. The table was too small.

I’d have to lay everything out on the floor.

I crouched down and placed the files in front of me.

All right.

First, the list of spies.

I opened the file and examined the table.

 LEAKED OPERATIVE RECORDS

W-001 — Matthew Collins — 1 April
W-002 — Nathaniel Reeves — 8 April
W-003 — Damian Voss — 15 April
W-004 — Elias Mercer — 22 April
W-005 — Sebastian Ward — 29 April
W-006 — Vincent Shaw — 6 May
W-007 — Nikolai Soren — 13 May
W-008 — Theodore Vale — 20 May
W-009 — Evelyn Frost — 27 May
W-010 — Isabelle Crane — 3 June
W-011 — Dominic Hayes — 10 June
W-012 — Julian Thorne — 10 June

Twelve names.

One leaked every seven days.

Meaning approximately three months of leaking.

Right.

I opened the calendar on my phone and checked the day.

Tuesday.

Every Tuesday.

Counterintelligence must’ve noticed the pattern and set a trap.

I picked up the manila folder labelled **#1** and spread its contents across the floor.

According to the report, their first trap had involved uploading new information on Monday. Each department received a different piece of information, allowing investigators to trace the leak back to its source.

A straightforward method.

And it had failed.

I flipped to the page containing the outcome.

The mole had ignored every single piece of bait.

Another operative had been exposed.

I stared at the report.

“…Of course it failed.”

The bait had been uploaded on Monday.

The leak always happened on Tuesday.

If I were the mole and brand-new intelligence suddenly appeared less than twenty-four hours before I was supposed to leak something…

I wouldn’t touch it.

It was risky. And probably a trap.

Counterintelligence had practically announced that they were investigating.

I checked the calendar again.

Thursday.

Four days left.

I put the first file aside and opened the next report.

This time, they’d placed the bait a week in advance.

The outcome?

Failed.

Of course.

I scanned the report again, looking for anything that might explain what had happened.

Then I noticed something.

This time, the mole had leaked two names instead of one.

I frowned.

So how come there were only twelve names on the list?

I placed the open file to my right and crawled over to the folder labelled **#3**.

This time, the investigators had altered existing information by the smallest possible amount.

The original code had been **12948**.

They’d changed it to **12943**.

That was smart.

If they’d changed the first digit, or replaced a number with something drastically different, a quick glance at the original information might have alerted the mole.

But three and eight were almost identical.

A subtle change.

Easy to overlook.

I checked the outcome.

**FAILED.**

Hmm.

What had the mole done this time?

I scanned the page.

No names had been leaked.

Interesting.

So that’s why there were only 12 names.

But why?

A pattern?

Did you need only twelve?

Were you sending a message?

Or had you simply decided it was too risky?

I sat back on my heels and stared at the reports spread across the floor.

Three traps.

Three failures.

And the third time, no names had been leaked at all.

The mole had either recognised the trap or had a reason to stop.

Neither possibility was particularly reassuring.

I looked at the calendar again.

Tuesday.

Four days.

And the bigger question was…

What would you do this week?
