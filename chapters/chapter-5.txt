 The Next Day — VIX

I walked past the Chief of Operations' office. In other words, Soren's office. I stopped by to greet him.

"Assalamualaikum," I said, peeking into his office.

His office, like all the chiefs' offices, was pretty big. But for some reason, Soren had kept most of it empty. Apart from his heavy oak desk and gamer chair, the only other thing that stood out was a bookshelf against the right wall, packed with books.

Soren looked up. He seemed tired.

"Waalaikumassalam, Jase," he replied, yawning. "Come inside."

I walked into the office and sat on the chair in front of his desk. Again, a gamer chair.

"Didn't sleep last night?" I asked.

He nodded. "Yes. The whole interrogation thing. I'm sure you have some idea of what's going on," he said.

Now it was my turn to nod.

"Yeah. Information's being leaked, right?"

"Mmhmm," Soren replied. "It's proving difficult to find the mole. And we need someone from Operations or Personnel to help."

"Why?" I asked. "I mean, shouldn't the Counterintelligence Division take care of it?"

Soren picked up a pen and started scribbling on the paper in front of him.

"Ah, yes, they should. But every time we laid out a trap for the mole... it failed. So we have reason to suspect the mole has an ally."

He'd made a huge circle of scribbles by now.

So the investigators themselves are suspects...

"So you'd need someone you know isn't the mole to take on this case. But since you can't find anyone, you're doing this yourself, which is hard enough as it is. And you also have your own duties as chief," I guessed.

Soren nodded for the fourth or fifth time.

"You know, someone like..." He trailed off, thinking.

I didn't want to hear his guess.

I quickly got up.

"Anyway," I said with a smile. "I hope you find someone to deal with the case."

And I quickly escaped from his office.

I got into my office and shut the door.

Finally.

I got onto my wheely chair and spun around a bit.

This mole guy either has a lot of friends... or they're smart enough to make VIX suspect everyone. Maybe that's just the baseline for how smart you need to be to become a mole.

Because if that person was framed, that would've taken a lot of effort too.

Buzz.

I stopped spinning, got off the chair, and walked over to the phone.

Keith: Come to my office.

That's all it said.

Yet, I would go.

Thud.

The heavy metal door of Keith's office closed behind me.

Inside, Keith, Soren, and the guy who'd interrogated me were seated.

Keith's office was just as large as Soren's, but considerably more furnished. A massive black table occupied the centre, usually scattered with papers and two or three pens. To the left sat a big black leather couch.

And then there was the bookshelf.

Completely empty.

I wondered whether he'd bought it for decoration or simply enjoyed wasting money.

"Ah, Jase, you're here. Take a seat," he said.

Keith and polite gestures? That's new.

I did so, and he pointed at the guy who'd interrogated me.

"This is Jack. New Chief of Personnel."

I took a second to look at Jack.

Curly brown hair. Brown eyes. Soft face.

Yet... dressed like Keith. Exactly.

I think one Keith is more than enough.

"Now, back to the point," Keith began again. "Remember how I told you to stay away from the case?"

He looked at me.

I nodded.

"Yes. I remember very well. If you want, I could quote what you said too."

This isn't looking good. Well, at least not for me.

"Yes..." Keith grabbed a mug and poured hot water into it. "Well, forget I said that. Because now..."

He took a green tea bag and dipped it into the mug.

"You deal with the leak. Find the mole. Arrest him."

What?

I stood up.

"No. I don't want to."

Keith stared at me for a while, dipping the green tea bag into the mug and pulling it out, then dipping it again.

"Sure," he said.

"No, I said I don't want to—"

Wait.

He said *sure*?

"But on one condition," Keith said, sipping his green tea. "Tell me someone else who can take it."

Hah. That should be easy.

I smiled.

"Leo."

Keith shook his head.

"No."

"What do you mean, no?"

"I think it'd be best if the mole didn't know who the investigator was. Leo is one of the few people in Personnel and Operations who's dealt with Counterintelligence. Therefore, he's an obvious choice."

I sat down on the sofa again.

"Well, what about Zane?"

Keith shook his head again.

"No. He's our prime suspect."

"Why?"

"Do you have someone else, or will you take the case?" he responded firmly.

"Ben," I said.

"Under suspicion."

"Adam."

"Dealt with Counterintelligence before."

I can't believe I'm saying this, but...

"Andrew."

Keith paused and put a hand on his chest, dramatically.

"Andrew..." he repeated. "He is a good option..." he said sarcastically.

Soren stood up.

"No. I doubt he could keep the investigation a secret. Plus, as it stands now, he's a suspect too."

Now it was Jack's turn to stand up.

"How is Jase not a suspect?" he asked.

Valid question.

Yesss! Jack, you're great! 

Soren and Keith went silent.

Keith then made a face. The kind parents make when their children start talking about mischief.


"Because he was our first suspect."

Jack frowned. 
So did I.

"He was framed once before, so we checked him before anyone else. He came out clean. The others are still pending, and we don't have time."

I leaned back.

Keith glanced at me.

"So, Jase, do you have any more names?"

I stared at the floor for a moment.

"...No."

"Then you have your answer."

I let out a long sigh.

"Fine."

A few minutes later, everyone left the room.

Except me.

Keith handed me a manila file.

"Take this," he said. "It's the list of leaked spies and information related to them."

I nodded and took the file from his hand.

"Keith," I began, "Soren told me that Counterintelligence set a few traps for the mole... which failed. So I'd like the reports on those too."

"Congrats. You finally said something that makes sense."

"Well, if I have a responsibility, I intend to carry it out," I muttered.

"Whatever."

Keith handed me two or three more manila files.

"Here."

He held up a finger.

"Don't. Lose them."

I nodded and walked out.

I got into my office and slammed the door shut.

I looked at the files in my hands. Then at my table. Then at the floor. Then at the folders once more.

Yeah, the table's too small. I'll lay them out on the ground.

Okay. First, the list of spies...

DESIGNATION - IDENTITY - DATE OF EXECUTION

W-001 - Matthew Collins - 1 April  
W-002 - Nathaniel Reeves - 8 April  
W-003 - Damian Voss - 15 April  
W-004 - Elias Mercer - 22 April  
W-005 - Sebastian Ward - 29 April  
W-006 - Vincent Shaw - 6 May  
W-007 - Nikolai Soren - 13 May  
W-008 - Theodore Vale - 20 May  
W-009 - Evelyn Frost - 27 May  
W-010 - Isabelle Crane - 3 June  
W-011 - Dominic Hayes - 10 June  
W-012 - Julian Thorne - 10 June

Okay. Twelve names. One leaked every seven days. Meaning approximately three months of leaking.

Right...

I opened the calendar and checked the day.

Tuesday.

Every Tuesday.

Counterintelligence must've seen the pattern and set a trap.

I took the manila folder labelled #1 and laid its pages out on the floor.

So their first trap was to upload new information on Monday. A different piece of information for every department to view, so they could map out where the mole was...

And it failed.

I flipped the page to the outcome.

The mole ignored every single piece of bait.

Another operative was exposed.

I stared at the report.

"...Of course it failed."

The bait was uploaded on Monday.

The leak always happened on Tuesday.

If I were the mole and brand-new intelligence suddenly appeared less than twenty-four hours before I was supposed to leak something...

I wouldn't touch it. It's risky. And probably a trap.

Counterintelligence had just announced that they were investigating.

I looked at the calendar.

Thursday. Four days left.

I put that file to the side and opened the next report.

This time, they placed the bait a week in advance.

Outcome: FAILED.

Of course.

I looked at the page for a few seconds and read something interesting.

This time, the mole leaked two names, not one.

So how come there are only twelve names on the list?

I put the open file to my right, then crawled over to get the file labelled #3.

This time, they just edited pre-existing information by the slightest amount.

The spy's code was 12948, and they changed it to 12943.

That's smart.

If they'd changed the first number, or a number that was drastically different, a simple glance at the original could make the mole suspicious.

But three and eight are almost identical.

Outcome: FAILED.

Hmm.

What did you do this time?

No names leaked this time...

Interesting.

But why?
A pattern?

You need only twelve?

Sending a message?

Too risky?

And the bigger question is...

What will you do this week?
