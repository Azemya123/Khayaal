We sat in silence for a while.

Either that 'mole'  had been really careless… or  masterfully framed.

Leo glanced at his watch.

I was surprised he even had one.

“Where’s Zane? He should be here by now.”

“Uhhh… yeah, let me ask him.”

I pulled out my phone to message him. But before I could type anything, I spotted Zane hurrying towards our table. He stopped in front of us, bent forward slightly, and caught his breath.

“Sorry. Sorry, guys. I kinda got stuck because of traffic and everything.” He straightened up. “And I met a few friends on the way and chatted with them, so yeah, that took time too.”

Leo smiled. “Yeah, sure. It’s fine. Just sit down.”

Of course he thought it was fine. He was always late without reason.

Zane dropped into the empty seat and glanced at me. Then his expression changed, as if he’d just remembered something important.

Which probably wasn’t important.

“Right, Jase. You asked for some…” He chuckled, raising his hands to make quotation marks. “‘General knowledge.’”

He laughed again, leaning back in his chair.

I stared at him.

“What?” he asked, still grinning.

“Nothing. Get on with it.”

His grin lingered for another moment before fading. He leaned forward, resting his forearms on the table.

“Right. Adam’s uncle…” He paused, searching for the right words. “Uhhh, how do I say this?”

“Just say it.”

“Basically, he was accused of spying on behalf of Varkov. VIX.”

The humour disappeared from his face.

…

Everything clicked into place.

Someone inside VIX was leaking spy names.

And VIX was stressing over it. And therefore dealing with it horribly.

I glanced at Leo. He'd stopped drinking his mocha. His eyes were fixed on Zane, his earlier amusement gone.

Zane looked at me.

“Jase… you realise what’s happening, right?”

I said nothing. One yes could become a problem for me now.

He shifted in his seat, his fingers tapping once against the table before going still.

“VIX is dealing with the mole pretty badly. It’s clear. And since it’s going to take them time to act, the result is obvious.”

He didn’t state the result.

He didn’t need to.

But emotional blackmail wouldn’t work on me.

“So?” I asked.

Zane stared at me. His jaw tightened as he read the challenge in my eyes.

“What do you mean, so? Come on, you’re not that dumb. You get what I’m saying.”

“Do I?” I replied. “Or are you expecting me to?”

Leo glanced between us, then slowly lifted his mug.

“I think it’s better if I just focus on my mocha for now,” he said, taking another sip.

A wise decision.

Zane ignored him, keeping his gaze locked on me.

“If they’re leaking names… then more people are going to die.”

Silence settled over the table.

Even Leo lowered his mug.

“You have to take this case,” Zane finally said.

I had only one reply.

“No.”

Zane blinked.

“No…?” he repeated, as though he’d misheard me. “Jase, people will die unless someone steps up to solve this.”

“So why don’t you step up?” I snapped.

Anyone else. He could ask anyone else. But he chose to annoy me.

Zane’s expression hardened briefly before he looked down at the table.

“I would if I could,” he said softly.

“But I can’t,” he muttered.

For once, he didn't seem to have anything else to say. Then he looked back up.

“By the way, what happened to being selfless? Or having a sense of patriotism?”

Patriotism?

Selfless…

“Look,” I began, “I’m not a hero. Or a particularly patriotic person. So no. I won’t take it.”

Zane held my gaze for a moment longer, then looked away. His shoulders sank slightly.

“Right… sure.”
