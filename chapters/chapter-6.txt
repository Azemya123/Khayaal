 VIX DETENTION FACILITY

"So, where are we going?" Zane asked.

Leo glanced at me before answering.

"Prison cell. We need to meet someone with firsthand experience in leaking names."

Zane went quiet for a moment.

"Carter?" he asked.

Leo nodded.

I watched the exchange through the window beside me. The tinted glass reflected parts of the car's interior, making it difficult to see anything beyond it.

The rest of the ride passed in silence, the only sound being the soft hiss of the air conditioning.

"We're here."

Leo's voice cut through the silence.

I got out of the car and shut the door with a thud.

The detention facility was deathly quiet. Our footsteps echoed as we made our way inside. After verification and clearance, we were escorted to the cell.

Carter's face brightened when he saw us.

Then it dulled.

The metal door rattled open.

We stepped inside.

It closed behind us with another metallic clatter.

"Assalamualaikum," Carter greeted us.

"Waalaikumassalam," I muttered.

Leo and Zane returned the greeting.

Leo spoke first.

"You leaked a few names once. Now, someone's leaking more names every Tuesday."

Carter glanced at each of us in turn.

"Every Tuesday..." he repeated.

His expression changed slightly.

"How do you know it's every Tuesday?"

"An operative was compromised every Tuesday. That's how," I replied.

"No." Carter shook his head. "You're confusing the date they were compromised with the date the information was leaked."

Interesting.

"The information could have been leaked anytime before the execution. A month before. Or a day."

"How long does the buyer usually take to act?" Zane asked.

Carter looked at him.

"Depends on the information. They might need time to verify it. Sometimes they'll monitor the operative first, especially if they suspect there are others."

"And sometimes they skip that part," I said.

Carter nodded.

"They already have a name. If they know enough, why risk waiting?"

I considered that.

"So the execution date tells us when they acted, not when they received the information."

"Exactly."

I looked down at the floor, thinking.

Carter leaned back slightly. "You came here thinking the pattern would tell you when the mole was leaking information."

It wasn't a question.

"We came here to find out what we were missing," Leo replied.

For a moment, Carter studied him.

"Then stop treating the Tuesdays as your starting point. They're the end of the process."

I didn't respond.

So the information could have been leaked long before the operative was executed. Tuesday wasn't necessarily when the mole passed it on. It was when the consequences became visible.

That made this investigation a lot harder.

But at least one assumption had been corrected.

---

 HOME

"So, now what?" Leo asked, leaning back on the couch.

I didn't know what to tell him.

Instead, I picked up a notebook and pencil from the table.

Summary

- An operative was executed every Tuesday in Asterra.
- The information could have been leaked at any point before the execution. Tuesday morning. Monday night. A month earlier.

I turned the notebook towards Leo.

"...That's it?" he asked.

"Unfortunately. But..."

I reached into my bag and pulled out the file.

"This is a record of the traps laid out for the mole."

Leo took it from me.

"Hmm."

He looked up.

"Okay. Take the pencil and write down what they did for each trap and what happened."

"But it's already written here."

"Yeah, but writing it out gives you a clear framework."

I sighed and started writing.

Trap One: Added new information on Monday.

Outcome: Other information leaked.

Trap Two: Added new information a week before Tuesday.

Outcome: Two names leaked.

Trap Three: Slightly edited pre-existing information.

Outcome: No names leaked.

I read it again.

Then I erased the outcomes and wrote something else.

Trap One: Added new information on Monday.

Outcomee: Another operative was terminated.

Trap Two: Added new information a week before Tuesday.

Outcome: Two operatives were terminated.

Trap Three: Slightly edited pre-existing information.

Outcome: No operatives were terminated.

Leo peered over my shoulder.

"Yeah. This is much more solid. No assumptions here."

I nodded.

Last time, I'd overlooked the significance of what had actually happened. Not again.

"What do you think the mole's motive is?" I asked.

Leo fell silent for a moment.

"Have you heard of Robert Hanssen?"

"The FBI agent who was a spy?"

"Exactly. His motive was..."

"Why not?" I interrupted.

Leo looked at me.

"He did it for the thrill," I explained. "Because he could."

"That's part of it, yeah." Leo leaned back. "But think about it. He kept doing it for years. Every time he passed information, he risked getting caught. And yet he continued."

"Until he did get caught."

"Exactly. And apparently, when they finally arrested him, he asked, 'What took you so long?'"

I raised an eyebrow.

"Imagine being that confident."

"Or that reckless," I said.

"Maybe both." Leo shrugged. "The point is, every leak is another opportunity to slip up. Every time this mole passes information, there's a chance they leave something behind. A mistake. A pattern. Anything."

I glanced down at the notebook.

"This mole's been doing it for three months," I said.

"Twelve Tuesdays," Leo replied. "Twelve opportunities to make a mistake, right?"

I nodded.

Twelve opportunities.

Had the mole made a mistake? Or was everything part of some plan? Some structure I hadn't identified yet?

I couldn't tell.

But I needed the next clue.

I stood up and dialled Keith's number.

"Assalamualaikum," I heard on the other end.

"Waalaikumassalam. Keith, I need you to suspend access to all operative files in Personnel and Operations. No one accesses them until Wednesday morning."

"What are you trying to do?" he asked.

"Find out whether the mole is still obtaining information."

A pause.

One.

Two.

Three.

"Okay. Consider it done."

Good.

If an operative died this Tuesday, that would mean the mole had obtained the information earlier.

If not, then that would make my job a lot easier.

It would be a good start and give me something to work with.
