  

I ran into the office in a hurry.

Forty-five minutes late.

I was a whole forty-five minutes late.

I was usually fifteen minutes late, but that was different. That was fashionably late.

Today, I was late by mistake.

“Ack!”

A voice cut through my thoughts.

I stopped and turned around.

A boy stood behind me, looking up at me with a frown. He couldn't have been older than fifteen or sixteen. He was short enough that I hadn't even noticed him in my rush to get inside.

Ah.

I may have bumped into him.

I hurried back.

“Sorry. Are you hurt?”

He frowned.

Okay. I hadn't expected a child to respond that way to an apology.

“No, I'm not. But tell me…” He paused, studying my face. “Why didn't you see me?”

I opened my mouth.

Then closed it.

The honest answer probably wasn't going to help.

“Why didn't I see you?” I repeated, buying myself a few seconds.

“Yes.”

He waited.

The way he looked at me told me I definitely shouldn't mention his height.

“Because I was—am late. And in a hurry,” I replied gently.

He considered that.

Then he asked, “Would you have seen me if you weren't in a hurry?”

The same expectant look.

I smiled.

“Of course I would.”

He seemed satisfied with that answer.

“Okay…”

He gave me a small smile and walked away without another word.

I watched him disappear down the corridor.

What's someone his age doing here?

It wasn't impossible. Leo and I had joined VIX around that age, after all.

But it was rare.

I turned back towards my office.

Forty-five minutes late, and I'd already managed to run into someone.

Wonderful.

---

 12 PM

Break time.

VIX headquarters was a maze of corridors and offices, with security checkpoints separating ordinary workspaces from restricted areas. Cameras watched from the ceiling corners, and agents hurried past with files and tablets.

It was surprisingly noisy for an intelligence agency.

The break area was even worse.

Conversations overlapped across the room, chairs scraped against the floor, and someone laughed loudly enough to make me look up from my food.

At the moment, most conversations had one thing in common.

'Why had we been interrogated?'

Everyone seemed to have a theory.

Unfortunately, most of them were stupid.

“Maybe some criminal is somehow a VIX agent.”

“Maybe someone didn't handle a case properly?”

“Maybe someone asked for an increase in their salary?”

The room erupted into laughter.

Haha.

I mean, most of the suggestions were stupid.


I returned to my food and let the conversation continue around me.

No one seemed to have any actual information. Just guesses, rumours, and increasingly ridiculous explanations.

 Monday — 12 PM

“Hey, I heard they've interrogated some people again,” Victor said.

I glanced up from my desk.

The conversation was taking place a few seats away, but nobody seemed concerned about keeping their voices down.

“Yeah, I heard so from a friend of mine too. He got interrogated another time,” George replied.

“Then that means VIX is probably suspecting more people,” Ryan added.

I returned to my food.

Another round of interrogations.

If they were questioning the same people again, they either hadn't found what they were looking for or weren't satisfied with the answers.

Neither possibility was reassuring.

Still, I didn't have enough information to draw any conclusions.

---

Thursday — 12 PM

“I think the situation's getting out of hand…” George began as he bit into his sandwich.

Adam looked at him.

“What happened?”

“You remember that friend of mine who was interrogated twice?”

George paused.

“Fired.”

The conversation fell silent.

For a moment, nobody seemed to know what to say.

Then Ryan spoke.

“Doesn't it mean it's getting under control? I mean, whatever the problem was, they got rid of it. Your friend was the problem.”

A few people exchanged glances.

George didn't look convinced.

Neither was I.

---

Next Wednesday — 12 PM

Adam walked into the break area.

Something was wrong.

I couldn't immediately put my finger on what it was, but he was quieter than usual. Normally, he was the sort of person who could turn an ordinary conversation into an event. He was talkative, energetic, and supportive.

Today, he barely looked at anyone.

Zane noticed first.

“Hey, Adam. Something wrong?”

Adam looked up and nodded.

“My uncle…”

His voice trembled.

The room gradually quieted.

“He lived in Asterra. Yesterday, I got news that he…”

He stopped.

George leaned forward slightly.

“He…?”

Adam drew a breath, as if trying to force himself to finish the sentence.

Then he said it.

“Was executed for espionage.”

Nobody spoke.

The word hung in the air.

I looked at Adam.

There were questions I wanted answered, but looking at his expression, I realised this probably wasn't the time to ask them.

Unfortunately, that realisation came a little late.

“On behalf of which country?” I asked.

Every head turned towards me.

I looked at Zane.

His expression told me exactly what he thought of my timing.

Right. Not the question to ask when someone's just told you a family member has died.

“Uh…” I cleared my throat. “I mean, that's horrible. It's really sad. And devastating. So, condolences for your uncle on my behalf.”

I managed to sound solemn.

I think.

The room's attention remained on me for another second or two before people began offering their own condolences.

After offering his condolences, Zane walked over to me.

“‘On behalf of which country?’ Really?” he remarked.

“I didn't think too much before asking,” I replied.

“Which sounds wrong coming from you.”

He shook his head.

Fair enough.

“Though,” I continued, “try finding out what country he was spying for in your own socially graceful way.”

Zane stared at me.

“And why?” he asked, putting his hands in his pockets.

I sighed.

“You know. General knowledge.”

“General knowledge? You can come up with a better excuse.”

He walked away.

I watched him go.

General knowledge.

Yeah, I could come up with a better excuse.
