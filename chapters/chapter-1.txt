
 The Beginning
Varkov, 11 AM

“Missed.”

“Missed again.”

“And again.”

“And again.”

“And again.”

“Twenty-seven misses now,” Zane announced as I hopelessly attempted to hit the target for what felt like the hundredth time.

Training. Apparently, spies being able to run was important now. Shooting practice came with it.

“It’s a bit too hot today,” I said, lowering my weapon and wiping the sweat off my forehead. “That’s why I can’t focus properly.”

“You say that while wearing a coat,” Zane said.

“It’s pretty thin.”

He gave me a look that said, *I could argue with you, but I won’t,* and pointed to the lane beside mine.

Leo.

He had joined VIX a few weeks ago.

“Bullseye.”

“Bullseye.”

“Bullseye.”

Zane counted them off with a grin. Annoying.

“He hasn’t missed once.”

Keith approached us. “You’re good at this,” he said.

Except it looked like he was saying that to me.

Even Zane looked confused. He pointed his thumb at me.

“Are you talking about… him?”

Keith nodded.

“How? How is Jase good at shooting? He’s missed 27 times. He hasn’t even hit the target board!”

“Yeah,” Keith replied. “When I said this, I meant missing.”

Thanks a lot for boosting my self-esteem.

After a few more rounds of missing, we finally headed back inside.

I walked to my office and opened the door, only to find a guy sitting at my computer.

I paused.

“Excuse me. This is my office. Table 29.”

He didn’t respond. His fingers moved rapidly across the keyboard as he clicked through one window after another.

“Hey, this is my table. You may have misunderstood.”

I made my voice firmer.

That got his attention.

Finally, he looked at me.

The man appeared to be in his late thirties, with curly brown hair and dark eyes.

“Jase Alexander.”

“Uh, yeah. That’s my name.”

“Where were you two days ago, on Tuesday, at 6 PM?”

Two days ago?

I didn’t even remember what I had for dinner yesterday. Though, the answer was probably…

“Home. I was home.”

“Is there anyone who can verify that?” he asked.

His voice was soft, but he seemed determined to sound authoritative.

And failing.

“Who are you?” I asked. “Am I the only one being questioned, or is everyone?”

He raised a hand, signalling me to stop.

“I ask the questions, not you.” He paused. “So tell me, can anyone verify that you were home at six?”

I sighed.

“Yes. My brother, Leo. And our apartment building’s CCTV.”

He studied me for a moment, then nodded and walked out, slamming the door behind him.

I dropped into my chair.

What was that?

I took out my phone and messaged Leo.

**Me:** Did they interrogate you?

**Leo:** Yeah. Zane too.

They had probably interrogated everyone, then. No harm in confirming, though.

**Me:** Was everyone interrogated?

**Zane:** You could at least add a greeting at the start.

Lunch.

I ignored that.

**Zane:** Not everyone. Only people in Personnel & Operations.

Interesting.

Personnel & Operations were the only two sectors that could access sensitive information. This wasn’t something minor.

If they were being investigated, it probably had something to do with information.

Meaning… something had been leaked.

It was a good guess, but I couldn’t be sure.

But what?

What had he been doing on my computer?

I opened it and tried to go through the files, but he’d closed everything he’d opened and restarted my computer. So… I couldn’t tell what he had been looking for.

My phone rang.

Keith.

“Hello,” I said.

“Jase. Don’t start investigating this.”

Great way to start a conversation.

“I was looking at my computer,” I said.

“Mmhmm. Definitely.” He sighed. “Do not jump into this. Leave. It. Alone.”

He hung up before I could say another word.

He hadn’t even specified what *this* was.

I leaned back and rolled around in my wheely chair.

Hm.

He didn’t need to repeat himself.

It wasn’t like I loved wasting my time and energy investigating some case. Especially ones I’d been told to stay away from.

**The next day, 12 PM**

I woke up to the smell of something cooking.

Not burning.

That’s… new.

I peeked into the kitchen and leaned against the wall.

“So you’ve decided to cook pancakes on a daily basis?” I asked.

“Yeah, pretty much. They’re easy and delicious,” Leo replied, his light brown eyes fixed on the round goop in the pan.

The goop was what he called a pancake.

Breakfast. Dinner. Pancakes. Pancakes. Pancakes.

I was tired of them.

I’d much rather have switched back to cereal and instant noodles, except Leo usually did the food shopping.

He finally took the last pancake and placed it atop the enormous stack of others. He then proceeded to pour syrup on them.

Too much.

So much you’d think it was a syrup fountain, like those chocolate fountains at fancy parties.

He handed me my stack and smiled.

“Enjoy!”

“Uh, yeah, sure…” I muttered.

We sat at the dining table, attempting to finish the pancakes Leo had made.

“I have to admit, these are pretty good,” I said as I munched on them.

“Mmhmm. You do have to admit,” Leo replied, covering his pancakes with more syrup.

I stared at him.

“Really? More syrup?”

His focus shifted from the pancakes to me, but only for a split second.

“The pancakes absorbed it,” he said before returning to his food.

I sighed.

“So,” Leo said, “what did you think about the whole interrogation thing?”

I cut a pancake into smaller pieces.

“Not too much. Keith called and told me to stay away from it. It’s about some leaked information,” I said.

“Leaked information…” Leo repeated thoughtfully. “No clue what the information is, right?”

I nodded.

“No clue at all.”
