 LOC’s Café

The café is busy for a weekday afternoon. The soft clatter of cups and cutlery mixes with the low hum of conversations, occasionally interrupted by the hiss of the coffee machine behind the counter.

Leo and I sit at a table near the window.

“One mocha, please,” Leo says.

“And a croissant for me,” I add.

The waiter nods, scribbles something on his notepad, and heads towards the counter.

I sigh.

“The whole interrogation thing…” I begin. “I'm almost certain it's due to an information leak. But now I'm starting to think spy names are being leaked.”

“What makes you think it's names?” Leo asks.

I glance around the café. Nobody seems particularly interested in our conversation. A couple sits at the table behind Leo, talking over their drinks, while someone near the counter struggles to get the attention of a waiter.

Probably nothing to worry about.

“Adam. His uncle was executed.”

Leo's expression shifts. The casual look on his face gives way to something more thoughtful.

“…For espionage?” he guesses.

I nod.

He leans back in his chair, his gaze wandering towards the window. For a few seconds, neither of us says anything.

He's probably trying to figure out what to do with that information.

I decide to change the subject myself.

“A few months ago, you mentioned that you and Soren investigated leaks abroad.”

“Yeah.”

“You never told me the full story.”

“It's a long story,” he replies, reaching for his phone as though he might find something more interesting to do.

He's trying to dodge the question.

Unfortunately for him, I'm not that easily distracted.

The waiter returns, carrying a small tray. He sets a white mug of mocha in front of Leo and places my croissant on a plate between us.

“Here you go. Enjoy your afternoon.”

He gives us a polite smile before leaving to attend to another table.

I wait until he's out of earshot.

Then I look back at Leo.

“Good,” I begin.

He puts his hand around the mug.

“I want the long version.”

“Sure. Any questions in mind? Because I'm not giving you a history lesson.”

Questions…

I lean back in my chair, considering where to start.

I smile.

“Too many. First, which country did you stay in?”

Leo lifts his mug and takes a slow sip.

“Which country did I stay in…” he repeats thoughtfully.

He looks towards the window.

Then back at me.

He's doing that on purpose.

“Leo.”

He takes another sip.

I stare at him.

He stares into his mug.

Finally, he sets it down with a quiet clink.

“Fine. When we got the detain-on-sight letter, Soren went to Keith.” He pauses. “Keith arranged a plane for us. Well… because he's Keith.”

I nod.

“Then we had to say goodbye to our country. Our home. Varkov.”

Leo places a hand dramatically over his chest.

“It was a truly devastating moment. In fact, I'd go as far as saying it was the most devastationous moment in human history.”

I reach across the table and take his mocha.

And drink from it.

“Mm.”

I take another sip.

“Tastes good.”

I slide the mug back towards him.

“Now quit stalling and get to the point.”

Leo looks at his mug, then at me.

For a moment, he seems to be considering something.

“…Right.”

Defeat.

“We went to Asterra. Keith wanted us to reinvestigate some really old leak cases. They'd already caught the mole, but Keith wanted to make sure they'd arrested the right person.”

“And?”

“We checked everything.”

He shrugs, as if that should be enough.

“Every clue pointed to the same guy.”

He reaches for his mocha again.

“End of story.”

“No. Not the end of the story,” I say, blocking his hand from the mocha.

“There's more.”

He looks at me without saying anything.

“If that were all, you would've just told me to read the file.”

The café continues around us. Someone laughs at a nearby table, and a chair scrapes against the floor.

“Something bothered you,” I say.

“…Yeah,” he finally admits. “Officially, that was the end of the investigation. Unofficially…”

His voice trails off.

I slowly push his mocha back towards him as encouragement.

“It never really sat right with me.”

“Why?”

“The man they arrested never changed his story.”

I frown. “Lots of guilty people deny it.”

“For a while,” Leo agrees. “Not for years.”

He looks down into his drink.

“Every interview. Every report. Every chance he got.”

Leo raises his eyes to mine.

“‘I didn't do it.’”

I study his face, waiting for him to elaborate.

He doesn't.

Instead, he looks away again.

“Keith thought maybe we'd find something everyone else had missed.”

“Did you?”

Leo slowly shakes his head.

“No.” He shifts in his seat. “We didn't.”
