 LOC’s Café

The café was busy for a weekday afternoon. The soft clatter of cups and cutlery mixed with the low hum of conversations, occasionally interrupted by the hiss of the coffee machine behind the counter.

Leo and I sat at a table near the window.

“One mocha, please,” Leo said.

“And a croissant for me,” I added.

The waiter nodded, scribbled something on his notepad, and headed towards the counter.

I sighed.

“The whole interrogation thing…” I began. “I'm almost certain it was due to an information leak. But now I'm starting to think spy names are being leaked.”

“What makes you think it's names?” Leo asked.

I glanced around the café. Nobody seemed particularly interested in our conversation. A couple sat at the table behind Leo, talking over their drinks, while someone near the counter struggled to get the attention of a waiter.

Probably nothing to worry about.

“Adam. His uncle was executed.”

Leo's expression shifted. The casual look on his face gave way to something more thoughtful.

“…For espionage?” he guessed.

I nodded.

He leaned back in his chair, his gaze wandering towards the window. For a few seconds, neither of us said anything.

He was probably trying to figure out what to do with that information.

I decided to change the subject myself.

“A few months ago, you mentioned that you and Soren investigated leaks abroad.”

“Yeah.”

“You never told me the full story.”

“It's a long story,” he replied, reaching for his phone as though he might find something more interesting to do.

He was trying to dodge the question.

Unfortunately for him, I wasn't that easily distracted.

The waiter returned, carrying a small tray. He set a white mug of mocha in front of Leo and placed my croissant on a plate between us.

“Here you go. Enjoy your afternoon.”

He gave us a polite smile before leaving to attend to another table.

I waited until he was out of earshot.

Then I looked back at Leo.

“Good,” I began.

He put his hand around the mug.

“I want the long version.”

“Sure. Any questions in mind? Because I'm not giving you a history lesson.”

Questions…

I leaned back in my chair, considering where to start.

I smiled.

“Too many. First, which country did you stay in?”

Leo lifted his mug and took a slow sip.

“Which country did I stay in…” he repeated thoughtfully.

He looked towards the window.

Then back at me.

He was doing that on purpose.

“Leo.”

He took another sip.

I stared at him.

He stared into his mug.

Finally, he set it down with a quiet clink.

“Fine. When we got the detain-on-sight letter, Soren went to Keith.” He paused. “Keith arranged a plane for us. Well… because he's Keith.”

I nodded.

“Then we had to say goodbye to our country. Our home. Varkov.”

Leo placed a hand dramatically over his chest.

“It was a truly devastating moment. In fact, I'd go as far as saying it was the most devastationous moment in human history.”

I reached across the table and took his mocha.

And drank from it.

“Mm.”

I took another sip.

“Tastes good.”

I slid the mug back towards him.

“Now quit stalling and get to the point.”

Leo looked at his mug, then at me.

For a moment, he seemed to be considering something.

“…Right.”

Defeat.

“We went to Asterra. Keith wanted us to reinvestigate some really old leak cases. They'd already caught the mole, but Keith wanted to make sure they'd arrested the right person.”

“And?”

“We checked everything.”

He shrugged, as if that should be enough.

“Every clue pointed to the same guy.”

He reached for his mocha again.

“End of story.”

“No. Not the end of the story,” I said, blocking his hand from the mocha.

“There's more.”

He looked at me without saying anything.

“If that were all, you would've just told me to read the file.”

The café continued around us. Someone laughed at a nearby table, and a chair scraped against the floor.

“Something bothered you,” I said.

“…Yeah,” he finally admitted. “Officially, that was the end of the investigation. Unofficially…”

His voice trailed off.

I slowly pushed his mocha back towards him as encouragement.

“It never really sat right with me.”

“Why?”

“The man they arrested never changed his story.”

I frowned. “Lots of guilty people deny it.”

“For a while,” Leo agreed. “Not for years.”

He looked down into his drink.

“Every interview. Every report. Every chance he got.”

Leo raised his eyes to mine.

“‘I didn't do it.’”

I studied his face, waiting for him to elaborate.

He didn't.

Instead, he looked away again.

“Keith thought maybe we'd find something everyone else had missed.”

“Did you?”

Leo slowly shook his head.

“No.” He shifted in his seat. “We didn't.”
